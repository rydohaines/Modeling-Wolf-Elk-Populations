# Modeling-Wolf-Elk-Populations
Project for Math 436
Math 436 – Portfolio Project Proposal
Team Members: Ryan Haines, Adam Eubanks, Jed Woods, and Norman Rasmussen
Our project will investigate predator-prey population dynamics using systems of ordinary
differential equations (ODEs), focusing on the relationship between grey wolf and elk
populations in North America. We aim to extend traditional predator-prey models, such
as the Lotka-Volterra model, to account for human influences on ecological systems,
particularly hunting and agricultural expansion.
Historically, human activity has significantly affected predator-prey relationships,
contributing to the decline of grey wolf populations across much of their natural range.
We want to investigate how introducing human influences into a predator-prey model
changes the behavior and long-term stability of the system. Specifically, we will examine
how varying levels of hunting pressure and other human-related factors affect
population dynamics, and how predator and prey populations respond to different levels
of human intervention.
We will also explore scenarios involving wolf population recovery and reintroduction,
examining how different initial population sizes and environmental pressures influence
the system's ability to reach a stable equilibrium.We intend to investigate the following
questions:
1. How does introducing human hunting pressure affect the equilibrium and
long-term stability of a traditional predator-prey system?
2. Under what conditions can hunting or other human influences cause predator or
prey populations to decline toward extinction?
3. How do different initial wolf population sizes affect the recovery and long-term
behavior of the ecosystem?
4. How sensitive are predator-prey population dynamics to changes in hunting
pressure and other model parameters?
5. Under what conditions can the system return to a stable equilibrium following
significant changes in either population?
We will evaluate our models using several measures. First, we will examine population
trajectories over time to identify trends, oscillations, equilibrium points, and potential
extinction scenarios. We will analyze the stability of equilibrium solutions and investigate
how changes in model parameters affect the long-term behavior of the system.
